# The Echo Below — Six Satellite Adventures

DM edition • Sites 3–8 • Companion to the Waterdeep hub

**Continuity:** the site names, order, suggested character levels, broad concepts, and starting conditions are established. **Everything else in these adventures is NEW campaign development:** room keys, hazards, named locals, handouts, rewards, mechanisms, and proposed consequences. These are original overlays, not published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* encounters. They do not replace its room keys or travel restrictions.

The Echo is resonance from the knot in the Weave. There are nine sites total, including Undermountain. These sites are **not a portal network**. Investigation never requires tuning or activating them. Halaster identified sites without personally visiting all of them. Looking Glass was designed to amplify the knot; its abandonment and the full consequences of completion remain unresolved. Do not use an invented local clue to answer those questions ahead of the campaign's established dungeon discoveries.

**Running these adventures:** each is approximately two sessions, expandable with travel and return scenes. DCs are suggested for the five-character party of Trista, Pebble, Donavan Kemp, Ringgo Paul, and Aldric Vane; reassess after major level or sheet changes. Checks buy safety, time, or detail. Essential evidence is available through a sensible action even when a roll fails. Describe hazards before their triggers; never trap everyone behind one failed roll. Damage occurs at most once per failed attempt, cannot repeat while characters pause, and can always be avoided by withdrawing. Clever equipment, a relevant spell, or a convincing physical solution can replace a roll. None of these sites requires combat or a specific class.

**Full-party integration:** at every site, offer different kinds of useful work without making any one character mandatory. Trista spots concealed access, evaluates valuables, and sees who bears a cost. Donavan establishes provenance, compares claims, and defines what evidence can support. Ringgo scouts routes, tests security assumptions, and identifies when an efficient method would endanger someone else. Pebble can examine stonework, brace equipment, and help others move safely. Aldric can compare magical dangers and choose restrained solutions without revealing his private past. Rotate which contribution opens the scene, and let collaboration replace a roll when their preparations genuinely solve the problem.

For each site, record its starting condition separately from the party's choice: unexamined, observed, deliberately tuned, isolated, or severed. No reward below grants new powers to Echo's Whisper. Inscriptions, copies, and local instruments provide alternatives if the medallion is absent.

## Site 3 — The Silent Library

**Established frame:** Waterdeep; dormant; suggested levels 6–8. This archive lies beneath The Second Margin. Its discovery confirms nine total sites. The former society's fate remains open.

**Objective:** recover a legible catalogue and understand what the Nine diagram records. Selise supplies the building deed and asks the party to preserve provenance: copy where each page was found before moving it. Donavan recognizes the Archive's same fieldwork principle - a discovery loses meaning when its context is destroyed - but the Library's notation is unfamiliar. A full afternoon of investigation suffices; waking the archive is optional.

> The stair ends beneath a shop that no longer seems to exist. Overhead, footsteps cease halfway across the ceiling. Dust covers empty desks and a circular reading table. Every book spine is blank. When your lantern moves, each chair casts two shadows—but only one follows the flame.

**1. The forgotten stair.** The deed locates a boarded service stair; workers can open it without a check. A quieter entrance runs through a disused coal chute. A DC 13 Investigation check identifies its brittle lower rung; failure costs a noisy landing and 1d6 bludgeoning damage, then reveals the same entrance. At the landing, chisel marks show that someone deliberately removed the society's name. They establish an omission, not who made it or why.

**2. The listening desks.** Spoken words become faint within arm's reach of a desk; elsewhere they sound normal. This phenomenon does not prevent verbal spell components. Written questions, gestures, or moving a desk work immediately. A brass reading arm moves when paper is laid beneath it. A DC 14 Investigation or Arcana check finds the arm's indexing rhythm; failure jams it until the party spends ten minutes cleaning it. Either outcome produces Catalogue Leaf Nine, handout 1. Its diagram has one center and eight satellites; the ninth identity remains unentered. No answer supplies Site 9's location.

**3. The circular table.** Eight removable name-slips surround a central depression. Most slips are damaged. The party can reconstruct the count by comparing their edges, without inserting the medallion. Copying the table reveals two separate columns: “recorded” and “sounding.” This establishes that a named site can be dormant. A DC 14 History check also identifies different generations of handwriting; failure leaves the dates uncertain, recoverable later through Selise. Nothing here proves a single builder made every site.

**4. The sealed folio.** A waxed catalogue packet sits behind a glass panel with a mechanical release. Opening the panel is harmless. Pulling the brass rod behind it begins an optional local test: dust rises and the desks repeat previously spoken words. The inscription warns, “A reading is not a summons.” The party can instead copy the folio through glass or dismantle the panel with tools. The packet contains a coastal bearing diagram useful to Vess and a torn description of pale ground in a living valley—supporting leads to Sites 4 and 5.

**Decision and consequences:** Selise wants supervised study; a mundane fire-and-water inspection makes clear that leaving fragile pages in place risks their loss. Move the catalogue to the shop, and study becomes easier but the archive loses context until carefully documented. Preserve it in situ, and spend money or favors on protection. Donavan can prepare an Archive-style field packet: copies, find-spots, and a chain of custody. That protects the evidence but raises an honest question about whether he sends copies to the Kemp Archive, given the risk that more scholars attract more attention. Optionally run the warned local test: it restores several damaged labels but creates an audible two-beat response upstairs, drawing a curious customer who may spread a rumor. This is a disclosed visibility cost, not secret empowerment of Halaster.

**Reward and return:** the party gains a reliable nine-site index, independent evidence that observation and sounding differ, and a secure research space if they arrange ordinary access with Selise. Let each player choose one harmless marginal curiosity to investigate later. At the Portal, Durnan asks what the quietest place in Waterdeep sounded like.

## Site 4 — The Drowned Observatory

**Established frame:** a wizard's centuries-old flooded coastal observatory; unstable; suggested levels 8–10. Halaster's warning reads “SITE FOUR / UNSUITABLE. / DO NOT RETURN.” The reason remains unknown. Its broken bronze dome lies beneath green-black water.

**Objective:** retrieve the observation log and determine why the ruin cannot hold a stable measurement. Vess provides true bearings once the party gives her a credible return plan. Her ship cannot solve the underwater portion. Make breathable air, swimming, lights, a tether, and a surface lookout concrete preparations; do not surprise the party with missing equipment after departure.

> A bronze curve appears below the waves, then disappears beneath the next swell. Through its broken ribs, stars glimmer in daylight. One is reflected twice. Your rope hums against the boat's rail, falls silent, then hums again a heartbeat later.

**1. The tide stair.** At the predicted low tide, a fractured service stair reaches an air pocket. The slower route follows the outside dome with tethers; the faster route crosses an exposed gallery. Show the cracked gallery supports before entry. A DC 14 Athletics or Acrobatics check crosses safely; failure costs 2d6 bludgeoning damage and leaves the character on a reachable lower ledge, not swept irretrievably away. Anyone following a secured rope crosses without another check. Both routes reveal the tide-height scale needed below.

**2. The keeper's room.** A dry cabinet holds the observation log in a ceramic sleeve. Patiently open the swollen cabinet, or succeed on DC 15 thieves' tools to save twenty minutes. Failure damages the cabinet, not the documents. Handout 2 shows that readings become unreliable at a repeatable waterline and preserves Halaster's warning. Comparison with verified dungeon examples confirms the annotation's attribution; until then, mark it as attributed. Its existence does not prove he stood in this room.

**3. The drowned lens.** A tilted bronze frame sits over a cracked observing floor. An ordinary tide floods two chambers unevenly; the moving water changes the frame's alignment. This explains some unreliable readings, not Halaster's warning or the site's instability. The party can sketch or take a rubbing from the safe perimeter. To reach a trapped salvage diver named Edda Marr, secure the frame with rope or make a DC 15 Athletics check. Failure shifts the load and inflicts 2d6 bludgeoning damage on the helper; Edda remains reachable through a crawlspace that requires abandoning her heavy salvage bag. She knows a safe exit even if distrustful.

**4. The counterweight well.** An intact tide wheel can hold the frame still for one short observation. Operating it requires two people or one person and a braced tool; no roll is needed after inspecting the linkage. Before use, show that the load will stress a cracked housing. The alternatives are a ten-minute measurement with the party holding the wheel, or leaving a replaceable brace to suppress movement until the next storm. Neither activates or stabilizes the whole network. The measurement records a paired response even after the lens is shuttered: reflected starlight alone cannot explain the Echo.

**Decision and consequences:** Edda's bag contains valuable mundane salvage but overloads the crawlspace stretcher. Save her first and return for it with preparations, or risk the exposed gallery with both. Afterward choose custody of the log: shared copies let coastal researchers improve warnings; restricted bearings reduce reckless expeditions. Vess supports publication of tide dangers while protecting the precise route. Neither approach stops future trouble entirely.

**Reward and return:** a brass star plate, the log, and Edda's testimony give three independently usable records. The plate is an accurate mundane observing aid once repaired; it neither finds sites nor opens travel routes. Orren can use its housing design to protect a delicate future instrument. Vess privately offers the name of the friend she lost here if the party treated another diver's life as more valuable than salvage.

## Site 5 — Dead God's Scar

**Established frame:** a pale wound across a living valley; corrupted; suggested levels 10–12. Divine contamination is present. The divine identity and any player-character connection remain undecided.

**Objective:** protect people suffering shared dreams and obtain evidence distinguishing local exposure from an outside attack. Anja's testimony, Ilvara's survey statements, or public travel records independently identify the valley. The settlement and residents below are new; adopt its name only if no existing campaign geography conflicts.

> Wheat bends in a warm wind until it reaches the white ground. There, every stalk stands perfectly still. Across the valley, the pale scar climbs a hillside without following the slope. A bell sounds from the village. For an instant, you remember being the person who rang it.

**1. Pale Ford.** Six households shelter in a grain hall. Caretaker Teren Moss refuses to leave bedridden residents behind. The dreams share sounds and gestures, but differ in faces; neither a dream nor this site supplies a deity's name. Compare three accounts and the party automatically identifies the same exposure window. A DC 15 Medicine or Insight check additionally separates exhaustion from deliberate exaggeration, saving supplies. Failure means a longer conversation, not false certainty or missing the lead. Handout 3 is a child's ordinary weather-and-sleep diary.

**2. The broken field channel.** A pale mineral deposit has crossed an irrigation ditch. Water and walking routes now carry dust toward the houses. Diverting the ditch, laying boards, and washing clothes reduce further exposure without “curing” divine corruption. A DC 15 Survival or relevant tool check organizes this before dusk; failure costs a day of work, during which the grain hall remains safe with its doors covered. No PC must become a sacrificial absorber. Cleared ground reveals old retaining stones, evidence that someone previously managed this boundary.

**3. The borrowed moment.** At the retaining stones, characters experience a brief sensory echo: heat on the left cheek, a hand braced against stone, a bell sounding twice. Ask each player whether to describe their character's emotional response; do not dictate a memory or secret backstory. A DC 16 Arcana or Religion check identifies the experience as repeating consistently when a stone moves. On failure, a second controlled observation confirms repetition while leaving its mechanism unclear. Careful mapping works without any religious expertise. The vision conveys no reliable identity, motive, or final answer.

**4. The exposed seam.** Beneath a collapsed retaining stone is a palm-wide luminous seam. A discarded survey flag vibrates beside it; moved ten paces away, it stops. The party can document the gradient from outside the bright area. Crossing the marked inner boundary without a rope or paired guide demands a DC 15 Wisdom save; failure causes 3d6 psychic damage and an involuntary step toward the seam, which a guide can stop without rolling. The character remains able to retreat. This is a one-time exposure event, not a domination effect. Temporary stone screens reduce the response locally; explain that a storm could dislodge them.

**Decision and consequences:** evacuation reduces immediate exposure but costs the harvest and may separate families from care. Staying behind screens preserves homes and work while requiring monitoring and a return plan. Combine approaches by moving vulnerable residents first and supporting a reduced work crew. Let the party allocate actual helpers and resources. Destroying retaining stones visibly enlarges exposed ground and is therefore an informed risky choice; it is not a hidden puzzle solution.

**Reward and return:** recovered boundary measurements show that local harm can be reduced without activating the site or proving the Still Hand's entire argument. Teren supplies food, lodging, and an honest future witness. The party carries a practical protection method to other investigations, limited to loose contamination and measured shielding—not a universal divine cure. Back at Lantern House, Anja listens closely to who stayed, who moved, and who accepted responsibility for returning.

## Site 6 — The Frozen Door

**Established frame:** northern glacier; sealed; suggested levels 12–14. An earlier expedition opened the lone black door; only one returned, repeatedly drawing one circle surrounded by eight. Someone other than Halaster sealed it. He was not the first to discover the Nine. The older seal's makers are not automatically the Still Hand.

**Objective:** recover the maintenance record and assess the seal before deciding whether reopening is justified. Kestrel's partial journal, its auctioned original, and the survivor's testimony independently provide the approach. Resolve provisions beforehand; Vess can arrange the first leg north, not arrival at the glacier.

> Snow has erased the mountain's ordinary edges. One rectangle remains: a black doorway standing in blue ice. Frost stops a finger's width from its surface. Beside it, a row of old camp rings lies empty. Every one faces away from the door.

**1. The expedition shelf.** The surviving journal identifies a sheltered traverse. Without it, cairns and a distant exposed camp establish the route. A DC 16 Survival check finds the efficient approach; failure costs half a day's provisions and reaches the same shelter. Do not start an unrecoverable resource spiral. Here the party finds a marked rope, spare mittens, and a note warning that smoke flowing toward the door signals unsafe wind. The expedition did not disappear merely because someone failed a check.

**2. The outward camp.** Researcher Ossa Fen has spent three days repairing a sled and can leave if helped. They want a readable maintenance plaque, not treasure inside. Melt its ice layer slowly, or use a DC 16 tool check to remove it quickly. Failure cracks a redundant corner but leaves every instruction legible. Handout 4 records deliberate sealing and exterior inspection. Its handwriting predates known Still Hand documents but establishes no exact age without further study.

**3. The inspection stones.** Three exposed markers flank the black doorway. They are exterior indicators, not keys that force it open. A thread suspended between two markers shows which cracked marker is vibrating. Repeating the test confirms that damage to one anchor has weakened local damping. A DC 17 Arcana or mason's-tools check identifies the most stable repair; failure still locates the crack but requires a second work period to test the fix. During a careless rushed attempt, falling ice deals 3d6 bludgeoning damage, avoided by using the rope shelter Ossa has already indicated. The maintenance record remains accessible outside.

**4. The face of the door.** The party can examine hinges, old patching, and tool marks without opening anything. They reveal successive maintenance, not an untouched primordial barrier. Readings outside suggest pressure rises during paired pulses; the source beyond remains unknown. The party can reinforce the external marker, leave a passive witness strip for a future visit, or propose a limited opening. A proposed opening is a separate expedition: require a retreat route, observations, and a way to close the actual seal before resolving it. No medallion touch automatically unlocks this adventure.

**Decision and consequences:** the repair uses a ceramic insert that Ossa hoped to study. Install it and preserve the present barrier; recover its full design first through rubbings and measurements. Take it intact instead, and leave the already weakened seal under a disclosed maintenance deadline after the next major thaw. Breaking the seal immediately is possible through a method supported by actual play, but the party has enough evidence to understand that it is an irreversible experiment with an unknown interior. Do not punish refusal with a mandatory opening.

**Reward and return:** the maintenance record supplies a tested model for reversible local isolation and a physical distinction between ancient workmanship and modern acquisition efforts. Donavan can compare the seal's notation to the Kemp Archive's traditions without declaring a link that evidence does not support. The party also earns Ossa's future field assistance and Kestrel's respect if they credit the incomplete journal fairly. Anja welcomes evidence that preservation and sealing can coexist; her faction's harder members may dispute its sufficiency without making her argument dishonest.

## Site 7 — The Walking Tower

**Established frame:** mobile; suggested levels 14–16. Its asymmetrical stepped crown remains recognizable across appearances. It contains a Looking Glass fragment. Halaster is trying to catch it; the party can arrive first. It is not a universal transport service.

**Objective:** predict an appearance, board safely, and recover the fragment with enough context to interpret it later. Selise's sketches, Pell's dated routes, and Vess's fisher witness independently identify the same tower. The party travels normally to its expected area; no Echo connection provides transit.

> Beyond the ridge stands a tower that was absent a moment ago. Its highest steps lean toward the wrong side of the sky. Grass lies flattened in a perfect ring around its foot. A window opens, and a strip of blue cloth flutters out—someone has been waiting for the ground to return.

**1. The arrival ring.** Comparing any two dated observations establishes a probable appearance window. A DC 17 Investigation or Survival check narrows it to one watch; failure means a cold night of waiting with a clear sightline. Before the tower changes position again, loose pebbles begin climbing its outer wall and the entry bell vibrates. These warnings give at least ten minutes to exit. This adventure takes place during a long enough stationary interval; do not move the tower merely to invalidate sensible preparation.

**2. The lower landing.** An itinerant conservator named Linn Aster entered to rescue drawings and stayed too long. Linn wants help moving two document chests down ordinary stairs. A narrow stair crack can be crossed with a plank or DC 15 Acrobatics; failure leaves someone hanging within reach and spills one chest. The documents can be gathered, costing twenty minutes. Linn can identify a Looking Glass inventory number but does not know why the larger project was abandoned. Their confident personal theory must be explicitly marked as a guess in dialogue.

**3. The tilted study.** Furniture is chained to the walls; its placement shows the room has tilted before. A brass mounting collar holds a curved fragment wrapped in old cloth. Handout 5 is the attached packing slip, establishing its provenance without explaining the full machine. Three disconnected measuring needles respond together when a tuning fork is struck beside the fragment. Document this behavior locally. It does not itself prove amplification; reserve that conclusion for the established deep-dungeon evidence. A DC 17 Arcana check distinguishes the fragment's response from room vibration; failure means repeating the test after padding the table.

**4. The counterweight room.** The collar currently acts as a mundane counterweight in a later repair. An engraved mass mark and spare stone blocks make the consequence visible: simply pulling it free would tip the study floor. Replace its mass, brace the floor, or leave the fragment and take precise rubbings. A DC 16 Athletics or tool check moves a substitute quickly; failure tips loose furnishings and causes 3d6 bludgeoning damage to an unbraced helper. A slow pulley method takes thirty minutes and requires no roll. Successful extraction does not control the tower's movements or create a travel command.

**Decision and consequences:** the next movement warning arrives after the party has time for one final careful operation: finish hauling Linn's heaviest chest or rescue an additional set of fragile diagrams. They may organize extra help in advance and save both. Prioritize the chest and preserve ordinary families' property records; choose diagrams and preserve technical context. This is a preparation problem, not an unavoidable sacrifice. Linn is upset by lost records but can be persuaded through honest explanation, never a single roll that erases the loss.

**Reward and return:** the fragment, its measured copy, or a documented decision to leave it all count as success. Selise gains provenance; Orren can examine construction techniques. Record custody and any later deliberate experiment separately. At home, Kestrel asks whether getting somewhere no one else has been matters more than bringing back something others can use.

## Site 8 — The Broken Star

**Established frame:** planar investigation; extremely unstable; suggested levels 16–18. Fractured light, strange gravity and distance, overlapping rooms, and an impossible view of Waterdeep suggest the Nine have implications beyond one plane. Clues establish the need for investigation, not the means of travel.

**Objective:** obtain comparable observations from a safe approach and determine how a proposed intervention changes local instability. Before starting, establish a legitimate outward journey and return method using the party's actual capabilities or a negotiated ally. Honor the chosen travel method's limits and costs. This adventure supplies no new universal portal or automatic medallion destination.

> A broken star hangs below your feet and above the ridge ahead. Its pieces illuminate one another with delayed light. Along the shore of empty space, stones cast shadows toward three different horizons. Your companion raises a hand. Their shadow lowers its own a moment later.

**1. The arrival shelf.** A stable shelf contains weathered tether points and handout 6, an earlier survey's brass index. Its author's fate remains unknown. Mark the return point before approaching the star. A DC 17 Survival or Arcana check identifies the indexed path; failure costs an hour of mapping and reveals the same route. No failed check scatters the party across planes. A visible crack grows whenever two fragments align, signaling the approach hazard.

**2. The contrary path.** Two routes reach the observation ridge: a narrow anchored traverse or a broad reflective slope. The traverse requires rope and patient movement, with no check after secure preparation. The slope saves time but imposes a DC 17 Dexterity save at one announced shift; failure causes 4d6 force damage and slides the traveler into a shallow rock basin where companions can lower a rope. The basin is a recovery position, not a hidden bottomless drop. Both routes expose a repeated change in shadow direction just before alignment.

**3. The broken instrument.** Two observatory rooms overlap; their doors show the same unreachable Waterdeep rooftops. These views offer no traversable route. An instrument compares three horizons using shutters. One shutter is jammed with star-glass: carefully clear it with tools or succeed on DC 18 Sleight of Hand to save time. Failure breaks a sample, requiring a mundane wedge. One slit gives consistent measurements; opening all three gives incompatible readings. Record both. This demonstrates a measurement problem without resolving the site's cosmology or Looking Glass's abandonment.

**4. The alignment interval.** The next visible alignment occurs after sufficient time to plan. Use three observations to predict it, or simply wait at the protected ridge and watch. A detachable shutter can reduce exposure at the ridge for the interval, making a second reading safer. Leave it in place to protect future visitors, retrieve it for research, or build a mundane replacement before departing. If characters choose to stand in the unshielded marked area, a DC 18 Constitution save halves 4d6 radiant damage from one pulse. Observation remains possible from cover; enduring damage yields no exclusive revelation.

**Decision and consequences:** a coherent star-glass sample could support a bounded later experiment, but extracting it loosens the instrument's oldest tether. This is visible under load. Stabilize with expedition gear, take only inert chips, or leave it and bring measurements. Record the instrument's resulting condition for a possible return. Deliberate synchronization is a separate, consented choice with a stated hypothesis and stopping rule; the adventure never treats carrying a sample as automatic activation.

**Reward and return:** the party brings back a repeatable warning sign, safer observation procedure, and evidence of how little one measurement can establish. Star-glass is an unusual research material, not an assigned magic-item bonus. Donavan can write Dianna a precisely limited account: observations, confidence levels, and no claims the party cannot reproduce. Her mathematical reply can later identify a pattern in their data without remotely solving the problem. Orren can incorporate the documented optical behavior into a later prototype chosen by the PCs. On Starcup Roof, Selise asks each character which uncertainty most changes their preferred next step. Preserve those answers for the late campaign; do not announce a predetermined solution or locate the Hollow Mountain here.
