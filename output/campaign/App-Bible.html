The Echo Below App Bible

Working rules for how the product prepares, runs and records a campaign. Campaign facts remain in reviewed continuity records; this document establishes no fictional event.

Audit the revised Session 4 · Audit Session 3

1. Preserve the table

Reuse existing state and services. Preserve saves, character statistics, media, map IDs, geometry, fog, explored objects and phone connections. Source books are read-only. Default rules are 2014 fifth edition with recorded character exceptions.

2. Approved layout

Table Launchpad is home; Scene runs conversation and narration; Map Desk runs combat and TV tools; Session Pilot and session exploration maps run exploration. Keep the map central and one contextual panel. Maintain Prep, Run and Review purposes without duplicating editors.

3. Every encounter has a purpose and handoff

Use Arrive → Describe → Respond → Resolve → Continue. Each scene defines purpose, entry condition, two paragraphs of observable narration, one useful question, likely approaches, earned disclosures, success/partial success/failure/withdrawal, connected exits, staged assets, sources and actual-outcome prompts.

An encounter ends when its question is answered, changes or is intentionally postponed. Combat is a possible development within a scene, not an obligatory separate story. Room order does not dictate travel; published geometry and actual decisions do.

4. Connected choices

Every exit names the destination, explains why it makes sense, states the prerequisites and supplies an adaptable transition. Choose from known information. Essential progress has several credible discovery routes; never make one failed roll or unavailable witness the only route onward. Do not move established evidence, invent NPC knowledge or disclose secrets to fix a stalled scene.

5. Human decision points

Preview → Show on TV remains explicit. Opening a scene or map is navigation, not a visit or publication. Resolve opens existing outcome review; it cannot silently spend money, grant loot, complete a quest or invent history. Temporary rulings have a source, explanation and follow-up review.

6. Audience boundaries

Filter before transport. Main quests belong on the TV, shared side quests on shared phones, character quests and private clues on the owner’s phone. Keep Active and Completed views. DM and NPC knowledge, claims, inferences, future plans and confirmed events remain distinct.

7. Review and correction

Propose linked quest, NPC, journal, map and recap changes from one actual account with explicit audiences. Keep source IDs and history. Corrections are reviewable and undoable. After a scene, ask only relevant outcome questions; after an evening, gather the complete closing review.

8. Audit before wider adoption

Session 4 is the first application of this encounter template. Audit branches before applying it across all chapters. Test isolated saves, scene navigation, TV privacy, resource preservation and return state. Automated results are distinct from actual DM, TV and phone use. Preserve an original restore point before replacing a saved guide.

9. Live interface for encounter flow

Show one task at a time in the parchment Scene workspace. Keep connected exits in Continue; retain manual scene selection under Change prepared scene. The scene aim is distinct from the campaign quest. Primary encounter controls stay beside the narration, while the artwork column owns explicit TV publishing and exact player-view inspection. Off-line displays show the last presentation rather than claiming a live image. Exploration leads with the current scene; full scene search and preparation remain accessible in expandable sections.

Research basis

  • 2014 DMG pp. 73–81: objectives, NPC reactions, redundant clues and encounter consequences.
  • LMoP pp. 14–19, 24–26: town leads, knowledge limits and developments. Actual campaign outcomes override the baseline hostage situation.
  • Dragon Heist: purposeful scene connections; Mad Mage: geography, inhabitants and aftermath.
  • User-selected Reddit discussion: manageable preparation and learning through play.
  • User-selected beginner guide: notes, clear rulings, flexible preparation.
  • Sly Flourish’s Eight Steps: flexible preparation.
  • The Alexandrian’s Three Clue Rule: reduce information bottlenecks.

This contract is an original synthesis for this app, not a replacement rules book.
