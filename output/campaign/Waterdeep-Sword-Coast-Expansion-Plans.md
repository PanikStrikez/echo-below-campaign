# Waterdeep & Sword Coast expansion plans

Prepared September 28, 2026. Proposals for the existing Echo Below campaign; no campaign outcomes or app data are changed by this document.

## Recommended direction

Build a small, memorable home neighborhood in a larger working city, then connect it to the road and the dungeon. Use Dragon Heist's city material, Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide's regional setting, and selected Tales from the Yawning Portal expeditions. Keep Lost Mine → Waterdeep → Undermountain as the current campaign path.

The app already provides settlement records, NPC knowledge, shops, commissions, downtime, clocks, travel, private clues, character spotlights, and expedition staging. Populate and connect those features through the current scene panel. These plans do not require another row of permanent menus.

## 1. Waterdeep that changes around the party

**Foundation:** Dragon Heist chapter 9, plus its introduction and Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide chapter 2.

Prepare a city overview with the six principal wards, the City of the Dead, and clearly distinguished outlying areas. Begin with about twelve useful destinations, then reveal others through play. The first set should include the Yawning Portal, Trollskull Alley, the Market, a dockside meeting place, the Font of Knowledge, the House of Inspired Hands, and practical lodging, repair, and healing services.

Each destination gets:

- A player-safe description and map pin.
- A morning, afternoon, and evening situation; opening times are custom preparation unless the book provides them.
- One recognizable person, one ordinary activity, and one optional complication.
- Services, reviewed prices, current availability, and a reminder of the party's last visit.
- A short arrival narration, staged image, ambience, and source link.

Use the book's street signs, drays, guild trades, ward identities, shrines, and public celebrations as things the players encounter. A lamplighter, delivery dispute, shared meal, or neighbor greeting can convey the setting without a lore lecture.

**App presentation:** clicking a ward offers three useful choices: visit someone, obtain a service, or follow a known lead. The right panel stages one brief local scene. City time proposes changes for DM review; it does not automatically punish players or create new confirmed history.

**First deliverable:** a playable arrival evening and following morning, twelve destination cards, a small recurring cast, and a bank of short everyday scenes.

## 2. A home and neighbors the party cares about

**Foundation:** Dragon Heist chapter 2, especially Trollskull Alley and its business guidance.

Offer a choice of renting familiar rooms at the Portal or obtaining a property such as Trollskull Manor through an explicitly established agreement. The manor is not already theirs: its original acquisition depends on Dragon Heist events that this party has not played.

Useful published neighbors include:

| Place | Person | Proposed use in this campaign |
|---|---|---|
| Steam and Steel | Embric and Avi | Repairs and commissions; a natural conversation for Pebble about his hammer |
| The Bent Nail | Tally | Furniture, shields, practical repairs, making a home feel personal |
| Corellon's Crown | Fala | Remedies, potion availability, and a familiar neighborhood relationship |
| Book Wyrm's Treasure | Rishaal | Books and access to listed spells; a contact for Donavan and Aldric |
| Tiger's Eye | Vincent Trench | Optional investigation work for Ringgo; the proprietor's secrets remain private |

The existing Second Margin, Ninepin Works, and Lantern House remain custom campaign establishments. Give them distinct roles: Echo research, controlled artifact examination, and existing campaign relationships. Published shops can handle ordinary services without displacing those contacts.

If the party chooses Trollskull Manor, use Lif's haunting as a relationship and restoration opportunity. Keep a simple review of repairs, staff, costs, and improvements; make detailed tavern accounting optional. Dracuseth remains a city contact with agreed responsibilities.

**App presentation:** one Home card shows who's present, messages, commissions due, and the next useful improvement. During a return, show two or three meaningful changes drawn from approved events.

**First deliverable:** two lodging options, five neighbor cards, a property offer, and a first homecoming scene. No automatic property award or expenditure.

## 3. A social network with useful work and consequences

**Foundation:** Dragon Heist's factions, Yawning Portal familiar faces, chapter 2 missions, and selected city encounters.

Prepare three initial points of contact rather than introducing every organization at once:

- **Civic work:** a Lords' Alliance introduction, potentially through Sildar if his actual relationship and availability support it. Jalester is an appropriate published Waterdeep contact.
- **Information and protection:** a Harper connection through a recurring acquaintance such as Mattrim. Introductions and membership remain choices.
- **Expedition patronage:** Obaya Uday, whose published interest in Undermountain already supports the campaign's next stage.

Each offer explains the job, reward, deadline, requested discretion, and likely cost to someone else. Add legitimate work, ordinary friendship, and competing interpretations alongside suspicious patrons. Let factions act on information they actually possess.

Use one active city conflict at a time. Xanathar's organization is a strong candidate for a later bridge toward Skullport. Jarlaxle's political schemes or a noble-house problem are alternatives. A full Dragon Heist treasure hunt is a separate campaign branch: choose its season and main villain deliberately, and adapt encounters for the party's actual level and resources.

The Redbrands' role in Gundren's abduction remains established custom continuity. Do not make them servants of a new Waterdeep villain merely to connect everything. Hamun Kost's disappearance can generate a rumor or inquiry; it does not prove a larger alliance.

**App presentation:** NPC cards show public identity, known relationship, outstanding promise, and the next proposed move. Hidden identities and faction plans stay on the DM side. Players can request a meeting or accept a proposed job through existing phone requests.

**First deliverable:** three introductions, six adaptable jobs, one optional pressure clock, and a clear route back to the main expedition.

## 4. A Sword Coast with recurring people and regional differences

**Foundation:** Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide chapters 1–3 and selected background/organization material.

Expand the journey map along routes the party is likely to use. Waterdeep, Neverwinter, and relevant road stops come first. Amphail belongs on an intentional inland excursion; do not place every interesting settlement on the same coastal road. Verify geography and route distances before assigning travel times.

Make each prepared stop distinct through its work, trade, local concerns, and services. Neverwinter's rebuilding, Amphail's horse trade and noble interests, and northern dwarven craft traditions provide different experiences.

Prepare a small recurring traveling cast: a caravan organizer, a courier, a craft merchant, and another adventuring company. Names and personal histories are custom additions. Meet them again with changes that reflect elapsed time and confirmed events.

Use the Calendar of Harptos and the chosen campaign date to stage one relevant celebration. Waterdeep's Guildhall Day, Day of Wonders, or a local sporting occasion could offer enjoyable noncombat play. Choose the date first; do not advance the calendar merely to fire every festival.

Travel scenes should include business, hospitality, weather, and choices as well as threats. Offer express travel for uneventful legs. News should have a source and travel delay: a merchant may know a rumor about Phandalin, not everything the players did there.

**App presentation:** a route preview gives travel time, known stops, supplies, and one optional encounter. Arrival stages a destination card. After approval, completed travel advances the existing clocks and proposes relevant letters or commissions.

**First deliverable:** the likely route into Waterdeep, four recurring travelers, three detailed stops, and one date-appropriate celebration.

## 5. The Yawning Portal as an expedition hub

**Foundation:** Tales from the Yawning Portal introduction and seven adventures; Dragon Heist's familiar faces; the existing Dungeon of the Mad Mage package.

Give the Portal recurring evenings, returning expeditions, displayed curios, contested stories, and patrons seeking help. A rumor is an offer to investigate. Durnan's knowledge remains bounded; he does not explain the Echo or Aldric's return on demand.

The anthology contains separate adventures with setting-placement guidance. They are not seven floors beneath the inn. Keep destinations geographically explicit and identify any relocation as an adaptation.

| Adventure | Published starting suitability | Proposed priority |
|---|---|---|
| The Sunless Citadel | Four or five level-1 characters | Later side group, flashback, or substantially adapted excursion; Lost Mine already occupies this role |
| The Forge of Fury | Begins at level 3; can reach level 5 | Strongest personal fit for Pebble; optional dwarven expedition, with party-level review before use |
| The Hidden Shrine of Tamoachan | Four or five level-5 characters | A deliberate distant expedition with its own cultural and geographical context |
| White Plume Mountain | Level 8 | Later unusual relic-recovery adventure; rewards need review before joining the main campaign |
| Dead in Thay | Levels 9–11 | A major later arc if interest in Thayan activity grows; not a short tavern errand |
| Against the Giants | Starts at level 11 | Later regional crisis that can affect trade, settlements, and allies |
| Tomb of Horrors | High-level challenge; no single fixed entry level in the overview | Optional challenge with an explicit discussion of its lethal puzzle style |

Levels describe the books' expectations, not a guarantee for this five-person party. Full selected-adventure preparation still requires encounter, map, creature, reward, and retreat reviews.

**App presentation:** a rumor card opens a private expedition preview with location, expected commitment, readiness, patron, return plan, and rewards. Only the chosen expedition becomes a full prepared session package.

**First deliverable:** a Portal evening, a small rumor board, and a Forge of Fury feasibility outline. Prepare the full dungeon only if the group wants that detour.

Forge of Fury is the strongest character match, but a second lost forge immediately after Wave Echo Cave could feel repetitive. Introduce a modest craft-history lead first and let the group decide whether to pursue it later. White Plume Mountain's placement notes explicitly suggest the vicinity of Mount Hotenow, making it a useful future reason to revisit the Neverwinter region. Dead in Thay begins from the aftermath of an earlier Bloodgate conflict: importing it requires a clear explanation of those events, rather than silently adding them to the party's accomplishments.

## Integration and build order

1. Reconcile source references and the campaign's date, existing NPCs, rules exceptions, and established history.
2. Prepare the arrival evening and morning with a manageable set of locations and contacts.
3. Add the chosen home neighborhood and character opportunities.
4. Connect the actual road journey, letters, festivals, and city-return changes.
5. Prepare one optional expedition or city intrigue branch when the players express interest.

Every new card should identify published setting material, custom preparation, rumor, and played outcome separately. Book narrators such as Volo and SCAG's travelers have perspectives; their claims need not become omniscient campaign truth. Published books also disagree on some shared details, including the Portal's well depth. Use the current Dungeon of the Mad Mage expedition as the operational reference for that entrance and record the chosen value once.

Maintain the campaign's 2014 rules default and existing character exceptions. SCAG character options overlap material in other owned books and include older versions. Adding the setting does not automatically change anyone's sheet or make every printed variant available.
