They supersede older open questions and provisional future-story proposals below on those topics. Actual-play records and current character sheets still control completed events and statistics.

DM-only preparation. Updated 9 October 2026. Every new scene, NPC, letter, and outcome below is a proposal until played and approved in campaign continuity. Current checkpoint: the party stopped at Barthen’s Provisions after leaving Gundren at an unnamed inn; Trista holds Glasstaff’s rat, and his castle destination is DM-only.

## Running personal scenes

Offer one or two deeper personal scenes per session and short invitations for the others. Rotate who receives a substantial choice; do not force five monologues into every encounter. Ask about interests, let players decline, and never confuse an offered hook with a played scene. No tracker counts have been invented for past sessions.

Start with the next player who has had fewer played opportunities in the selected session. Count a meaningful decision, a discovery they engage with, or a relationship they develop—not just a speech or a combat kill. Absence and declining a theme are not failures.

The DM tracker records offered / played / deferred / declined separately. Mark played with an actual outcome; then optionally create an unapproved private continuity draft and choose who learned what before approval. The tracker never changes character mechanics, reveals clues, or posts to phones automatically.

## Aldric and Halaster: custom DM truth

Halaster’s experimental return lattice restored Aldric as a Reborn after the Crown killed him. The ring was an identifying anchor, not a soul prison. Halaster’s experiment damaged continuity of memory; it did not create a right to control Aldric.

The Crown caused the execution and family murders. Halaster did not arrange them. The Crown’s identity, family names, and the intermediary’s route remain open. The return does not depend on continued service, a worn ring, or a still-functioning machine.

Halaster remembers an unidentified experiment subject, not a tracked adventuring party. An actual Forge interaction may allow recognition; otherwise delay until a credible later contact. This revises only Aldric’s open resurrection cause and preserves the campaign’s earned Waterdeep transition.

### Corroboration ladder

- A ring sketch, maker’s commission, and appointment roll establish identity.
- A dated experiment record is authenticated independently of Halaster.
- A surviving independent attendant or intermediary corroborates a bounded detail.
- Only then treat Halaster’s role as confirmed in character knowledge; memories and boasts alone remain claims.

## Trista

**Central question:** Who should hold a dangerous discovery, and what does trust require of its keeper?

**Already established:** Trista kept the BELOW token in Session 1. Donavan noticed concealment, not its identity. Gundren gave her a second chance. Her level-3 Shadow abilities come from her class; Echo’s Whisper is a separate, not-yet-awarded object.

**Interests:** secrecy, trust, Shadow abilities, custody, protecting others

**Guardrails:** No forced confession, theft, corruption, or party vote over her character. Refusing custody is a complete arc choice. The token does not grant new powers or make her class abilities an Echo effect.

### Redbrand hideout - The place you choose to stand

**Trigger:** Offer during the approach, then revisit only if the party actually finds prisoners or evidence.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> There is room to keep watch, scout ahead, or stay close to the others. Trista, who would you want beside you if something unexpected turned up?

**Private preparation:** Give a genuine scouting or protection role suited to her current Shadow tools. Do not put another BELOW token in this hideout. If she privately examines hers, repeat only its already-established appearance; invent no new compulsory reaction.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Keep the token private and ask for practical help: trust can grow without disclosure.
- Share a small observation with one chosen person: record precisely what they learn.
- Show the token voluntarily: let the group ask questions before discussing custody.

**Evidence:** A witnessed conversation establishes only what was actually shown or said. No Insight check identifies a concealed token automatically.

**Follow-through:** Ask who should hold any newly recovered papers and who may copy them. Record an actual agreement only after the players make it.

**If declined or missed:** If she avoids the subject, let her scouting, protection, or careful retreat be the spotlight. Carry the trust question forward. Do not count secrecy as failure.

### Phandalin aftermath - A favor with no price

**Trigger:** Use a quiet moment after a real act of aid in Phandalin.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> A person you helped offers a small place at their table. “I cannot repay everything. Is there one thing I can do for you?”

**Private preparation:** Choose someone actually helped, not an invented rescued prisoner. Their help can be a message, safe meeting place, or witnessing an inventory. They do not know about the token unless told.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Accept practical help: establish a voluntary contact.
- Ask them to help someone else: let that person decide what aid they need.
- Decline payment but exchange names: gratitude need not become a debt.

**Evidence:** The NPC’s witnessed offer and any agreed terms become the record; do not manufacture a debt for charity.

**Follow-through:** Let Gundren’s old act of trust become something Trista can pass on without copying his exact choices.

**If declined or missed:** If no one was helped, offer a modest paid errand with a fair wage. Avoid rewarding an invented rescue.

### Wave Echo Cave - The keeper is a choice

**Trigger:** Only after the party reaches the Forge and chooses to examine the actual discoveries.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> You can keep this where it is, carry it away, or make a record before anyone touches it. What would make you willing to trust another person with the decision?

**Private preparation:** If Echo’s Whisper is introduced, identify it separately from the BELOW token. No automatic transfer to Trista. Make any real handling danger explicit before the choice; no secret alignment test or invented attunement benefit.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Accept temporary custody with a witness and a return condition.
- Share custody through agreed storage and access rules.
- Decline and help choose another keeper or leave it secured.

**Evidence:** Inventory, location sketch, and a witnessed handover establish custody; resemblance alone does not establish a common maker.

**Follow-through:** Write the chosen keeper, access rules, and next review point into continuity. Offer a later challenge to those terms, not an automatic theft.

**If declined or missed:** A rubbing or sketch preserves the lead if the group leaves the object behind. Essential routes do not depend on Trista holding it.

### Waterdeep - Mara asks for the whole truth

**Trigger:** After Mara is introduced and a genuine discovery or custody arrangement exists.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Mara says, “Tell me what you can promise. I would rather know the limit than find it later.”

**Private preparation:** Use Mara’s established theft-contract pressure as an offer with a visible risk, not proof she betrays Trista. She must learn about any object through an actual conversation or source. Make a lawful transport/storage alternative available.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Disclose the risk but withhold the location: negotiate an honest limit.
- Show evidence under supervision: give Mara a chance to earn trust.
- Refuse the contract and offer another way out: neither friendship nor clues are locked behind theft.

**Evidence:** The written offer proves what was offered; Mara’s interpretation remains a claim until checked.

**Follow-through:** Let the next meeting reflect the specific promise kept or broken. Trista may change custody with the other involved players’ agreement.

**If declined or missed:** If Mara is avoided, Selise can present the storage question openly. No unseen theft punishes avoidance.

### Undermountain and the Nine - Privacy without isolation

**Trigger:** At the Silent Library or another site where copying and custody are genuinely in dispute.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> One person wants the original sealed. Another wants a copy available to anyone in danger. Both ask what you would need before trusting them.

**Private preparation:** Offer a concrete access agreement: who can inspect, who keeps the original, what can be copied, and when the arrangement is reviewed. Give Trista a useful route/scouting task in parallel; avoid making every Shadow scene a confession.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Keep the original private but release a checked warning.
- Use independent custodians and record access.
- Return it to affected people with an agreed inspection plan.

**Evidence:** Compare access logs with witnesses. A missing entry is a discrepancy, not automatic proof a friend stole something.

**Follow-through:** Consequences concern speed, accountability, and relationships. Protection should have practical benefits as well as costs.

**If declined or missed:** If the group rejects formal rules, record their simpler verbal agreement and revisit it after a real change.

### Late-campaign decisions - What you are willing to carry

**Trigger:** When the party has enough evidence for a major decision about Looking Glass or an affected site.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> You once kept a small thing because it was yours to understand. What, now, are you willing to keep—and what responsibility belongs with someone else?

**Private preparation:** Offer an ending grounded in her choices: trusted keeper, shared steward, protector who refuses possession, or a new path she proposes. Do not force the medallion onto her or equate openness with moral goodness.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Keep custody under terms others can question.
- Establish shared stewardship and a right to withdraw.
- Hand it over or safely relinquish it while preserving the warnings.

**Evidence:** Use actual agreements and observed consequences. The late-campaign mystery does not erase prior custodians’ decisions.

**Follow-through:** Epilogue: show one specific person or practice improved by her chosen form of trust.

**If declined or missed:** If Trista never reveals the token, close the arc through another real decision about secrecy and care.

## Pebble

**Central question:** Can you learn your own way through the world while still asking others for help?

**Already established:** Byron’s Goron comes from the mountains, loves rocks, fears getting lost, and follows a shiny-mineral story told by the dwarves who made his hammer. Champion, Crusher, and Interception remain his current sheet features.

**Interests:** hammer-makers, mountain home, minerals, navigation, belonging

**Guardrails:** No forced eating, damage from an unannounced tasting test, automatic mineral identification, or free weapon upgrades. Working names for new dwarves or his home remain editable until Byron establishes them.

### Redbrand hideout - Mark the way back

**Trigger:** Offer at the entrance before anyone commits to a route.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Pebble, what would help you recognize the way back? You have chalk, familiar shapes, and friends who can check the route with you.

**Private preparation:** Give him a real route-marking role if he wants it. Describe ordinary masonry or tool marks where supported by the room; the marks are practical information, not supernatural knowledge of every corridor.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Mark turns with a personal stone symbol: show those marks on the return.
- Pair up with a companion to verify each turn: shared navigation is progress.
- Choose a meeting point and ask someone else to lead: confidence can include delegating.

**Evidence:** A route sketch and physical marks can be checked by the party. Use normal checks only for genuinely uncertain terrain.

**Follow-through:** On retreat, ask Pebble to point out a marker he placed. Record a success if the player enjoyed that moment.

**If declined or missed:** If he does not want to navigate, offer him a protective role at a doorway and let someone else keep the map.

### Phandalin aftermath - The makers’ mark

**Trigger:** At a smith or trader only if Pebble shows his hammer.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> The smith turns the hammer carefully. “Good work. Who made this for you? What would you want them to know you have done with it?”

**Private preparation:** Let Byron name the makers first. Optional working names: Torra and Brennir Flintwell, two dwarven smiths, not secret Echo guardians. A trader can route a letter to them after Pebble supplies a real home/contact description.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Send a drawing of the hammer at work: invite a craft reply.
- Ask about the shiny mineral story: receive a lead, not a guaranteed location.
- Request a simple maintenance lesson: gain a relationship, not a new combat modifier.

**Evidence:** A maker’s reply can confirm their own workmanship and the original rumor’s source. The mineral’s properties still need independent testing.

**Follow-through:** Prepare a return letter praising a choice to protect someone, with a small route sketch or ordinary repair tip.

**If declined or missed:** If he keeps his home private, a local craftsperson can teach the same skill without contacting anyone.

### Wave Echo Cave - Something better than a shiny rock

**Trigger:** Offer when a safe, ordinary sample can be found during exploration; not in place of keyed treasure.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> A seam catches the light. Some pieces shine more than others, but the marks around it show people once worked carefully here. What would you like to learn before you take a piece?

**Private preparation:** Custom mineral proposal: sweetstone, a mundane calcite-like vein Byron may describe as a favored Goron food after safe identification. Distinguish it from charged residue at the Forge. No tasting is required and there is no permanent power reward.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Collect a small safe sample with location notes.
- Leave the seam and sketch it for the hammer-makers.
- Ask who owns the claim and arrange a fair share before extraction.

**Evidence:** Compare a known sample, a craftsperson’s identification, and the location record. Brightness alone does not prove safety or Echo resonance.

**Follow-through:** Let the makers react to what he learned as well as what he brought. An unusual mineral may matter because of craft, beauty, or a community’s livelihood.

**If declined or missed:** If the mine is unsafe or left unexplored, use a trader’s ordinary sample later; do not block his arc behind the dungeon.

### Waterdeep - Your own route home

**Trigger:** After the group establishes the Yawning Portal or another agreed meeting place.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> The city is loud, but three landmarks now make sense: the place you left, the turn you remember, and where your friends will wait. Which route do you want to show them?

**Private preparation:** Invite Byron to invent a harmless mnemonic and one favorite shop. A wrong turn costs a small detour with a helpful sign or resident, not separation, humiliation, or an ambush solely for failing navigation.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Lead the group along a familiar route.
- Build a pocket map with a companion and ask a local to verify it.
- Help another newcomer find their way.

**Evidence:** Let landmarks remain stable across visits; record new knowledge rather than quietly changing the streets.

**Follow-through:** Pebble becomes someone another traveler can rely on. Return to the same route after a later expedition.

**If declined or missed:** If urban navigation is not enjoyable, make the spotlight a warm craft-shop visit and keep routes simple.

### Undermountain and the Nine - A letter from the mountain

**Trigger:** Once a correspondence route exists; deliver between expeditions, not mid-combat.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> “We would like to know the places you have learned. Could you send a map someone else could follow? There is a young traveler here who is frightened of taking the first step.”

**Private preparation:** The mountain home needs a useful connection, not compulsory rescue from destruction. Offer a trade-route question or a craft commission. Do not make his whole tribe vanish to create urgency.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Return for a visit with a practical route and samples.
- Arrange a trusted courier and a fair mineral trade.
- Invite a visitor and prepare a safe route through Waterdeep.

**Evidence:** Confirm sender, route, and the actual request. Rumors of a rare mineral remain rumors until examined.

**Follow-through:** Connect one craft need to a site the party already chooses to visit; the home arc never assigns every expedition.

**If declined or missed:** If travel home would derail the group, resolve correspondence during downtime and offer a later visit.

### Late-campaign decisions - The one who knows the way

**Trigger:** At a chosen homecoming, rescue route, or late expedition departure.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Someone asks you the question you once needed to ask: “How do I get there without losing everyone?” What do you tell them?

**Private preparation:** Confidence is using preparation and asking for help, not deleting his fear. Let the hammer remain his makers’ work unless an actual upgrade is separately earned and approved.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Guide others and teach them to check the route themselves.
- Build a lasting connection between home and the wider world.
- Choose a smaller familiar life and visit his friends by reliable routes.

**Evidence:** Use real routes, correspondence, and acts of protection from the tracker.

**Follow-through:** Epilogue: a new traveler recognizes Pebble’s trail mark, or the makers hang his map beside their forge.

**If declined or missed:** If his interests shift, close with a craft or friendship accomplishment rather than a navigation speech.

## Donavan Kemp

**Central question:** How can you make knowledge useful without surrendering the people it might harm?

**Already established:** Brodey’s researcher belongs to the Kemp Archive in an elven metropolis. Dianna, his wife, is a mathematician; Favian, Vivian, Lilian, Conner, and Nilla are family. Markus is dead, but the truth remains open. Donavan is now an Order of Scribes Wizard 2 / Knowledge Cleric 1.

**Interests:** research, family letters, rival interpretations, publication, provenance

**Guardrails:** Do not make every family member a hostage or every rival dishonest. The Archive is not in the Fey Realm. Publishing a claim does not make it true; Markus’s fate is not automatically tied to Halaster.

### Redbrand hideout - The useful discrepancy

**Trigger:** Only when real hideout papers or testimony are found; an added scrap is clearly a custom prop.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Two accounts agree on a name but disagree about what it means. What would you need to record before deciding which one to trust?

**Private preparation:** Use the distinction between the party’s Redbrand lead and any newly found transfer evidence. Do not place Gundren here or give a castle location without a credible discovered source. Let Donavan label who said what and when.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Copy exact wording and preserve the original separately.
- Ask another PC to independently inspect a seal or route.
- Keep the conclusion open and record the next check needed.

**Evidence:** Two copies of the same letter are one source. A witness with direct access and an independently dated record can corroborate a transfer.

**Follow-through:** A correct uncertainty can protect the party from a rushed conclusion. Give the player the usable record even on a failed roll; a check may add context.

**If declined or missed:** If the papers are destroyed, compare independent testimony later. The main Gundren rescue lead remains available through adventure routes.

### Phandalin aftermath - Dianna’s first reply

**Trigger:** During a quiet rest in town after a message could reasonably arrive.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> “Donavan—send the ordinary details too. What did you eat, and who made you laugh? For the measurements, keep the result you expected separate from the one you found. I would like both versions. Love, Dianna.”

**Private preparation:** This is an optional new letter from his established wife. Include a mundane family detail Brodey supplies. Her request is a matched comparison of an unusual site with an ordinary one, not a remote solution to the Nine.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Reply personally before writing the report.
- Send a cautious observation with the uncertainty intact.
- Ask Dianna a mathematical question and propose a safe comparison.

**Evidence:** Her calculation can test a submitted model, but it cannot verify an observation she did not witness.

**Follow-through:** Let the reply cite Donavan’s actual question and one other PC’s contribution. Keep family correspondence reciprocal.

**If declined or missed:** If the player dislikes letter scenes, summarize the message in a sentence and offer it for later reading.

### Wave Echo Cave - A finding someone can reproduce

**Trigger:** After an actual Forge investigation; avoid dangerous tuning merely to earn data.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> You can record where you stood, what happened, and what changed. Which comparison would help someone distinguish an ordinary magical effect from your explanation?

**Private preparation:** Keep the Forge’s published magic separate from the Echo overlay. Introduce a proposed academic rival, Ilyra Voss, through an earlier paper arguing residual enchantment explains such effects. She has a testable hypothesis, not secret villain status.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Send a narrow observation with no claim of cause.
- Arrange a safe repeat measurement with a witness.
- Write both hypotheses and a test that could prove your own wrong.

**Evidence:** Different observers, a control location, timestamps, and independent records strengthen a claim. Agreement between friends alone is not proof.

**Follow-through:** The Archive can ask a follow-up that changes with the data. A disputed explanation need not invalidate the party’s observations.

**If declined or missed:** If no repeat is safe, publish the limit explicitly. Not experimenting can be a defensible research result.

### Waterdeep - The redacted report

**Trigger:** When Ilvara, Selise, or an Archive correspondent requests the research.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> “I can copy what you send,” the correspondent says. “Tell me who could be harmed by the details, and which conclusions you are ready to put your name beside.”

**Private preparation:** Offer public summary, restricted review, and delayed publication. Give each a concrete cost: recognition/access, independent scrutiny, or speed of warning. No option is automatically the moral one.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Publish observations while redacting dangerous access instructions and witness identities.
- Invite independent reviewers under agreed handling terms.
- Delay release while sending a practical warning to affected people.

**Evidence:** Keep an original, date the release, credit collaborators, and distinguish redaction from altering evidence.

**Follow-through:** Have a reader use the actual published version. Later requests must respect what was withheld and who received it.

**If declined or missed:** If no one has requested a report, let Donavan initiate one; do not conjure a deadline or punishment for silence.

### Undermountain and the Nine - A rival changes one of your conclusions

**Trigger:** At an appropriate field site or a Waterdeep return after new observations.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Ilyra sets two records beside yours. “This part supports you. This part does not. Will you help me find out why?”

**Private preparation:** Prepare one bounded discrepancy, such as a clock offset or unmatched location. Decide the real cause before presenting it. Let independent checks show it. Ilyra can be wrong about the wider theory and right about a detail.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Correct the public claim and credit the correction.
- Run a jointly designed safe test with the party.
- Keep disagreement visible while acting on the facts both sides accept.

**Evidence:** A correction needs its own traceable evidence. Do not change the answer to defeat the player’s successful test.

**Follow-through:** Trust can grow through honest disagreement. Family may celebrate his integrity without knowing every private expedition detail.

**If declined or missed:** If the party avoids the rival, another independent review or their own notes can expose the discrepancy.

### Late-campaign decisions - The archive you leave behind

**Trigger:** Before the final consequential publication, sealing, or stewardship decision.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> Your record could outlive everyone who remembers why it was written. What must a future reader be able to check—and what do they need permission to use?

**Private preparation:** Offer an accountable public archive, a community-held collection with access rules, or a carefully limited warning. Keep independent records available so Donavan is not the sole arbiter of truth.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Publish with provenance, corrections, and safety limits.
- Place originals with multiple accountable custodians.
- Withhold a dangerous method while making its risks and the reason for withholding reviewable.

**Evidence:** Build the final record from actual evidence and credit each PC’s work. Do not manufacture a final proof about Markus or the Weave.

**Follow-through:** Epilogue: a student, family member, or affected community uses his honest account to make their own decision.

**If declined or missed:** If research stopped being his main interest, focus on one truthful letter home and a concrete person he helped.

**Interests:** old contacts, unfinished business, honest work, restraint, chosen identity

**Trigger:** When the party can scout a real room or speak to an opponent who has a credible reason to yield.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> You notice where someone would run and what would make surrender believable. What would you need to offer for this to end without another body?

**Private preparation:** Use actual sightlines and morale. Do not guarantee a surrender roll, but explain credible conditions: distance, disarmament, a clear exit, or a witness. Let Ringgo investigate a bluff rather than auto-detecting one.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Arrange a witnessed surrender with concrete terms.
- Create a diversion or escape route for a vulnerable person.
- Use force if necessary while choosing a limit and honoring it.

**Evidence:** Record what the opponent actually promised and whether they had the ability to keep it. Surrender does not make every statement true.

**Follow-through:** An honest reputation can begin with one reliable agreement. Do not invent saved prisoners if none were found.

**If declined or missed:** If negotiation is impossible, spotlight reconnaissance that reduces risk for the group. A combat outcome does not fail his story.

- Offer a safer plan that costs the client less.

**Evidence:** A receipt, payment, and named witness establish the result; reputation grows from acts people actually know about.

## Aldric Vane

**Central question:** What does a second life let you repair, and who has the right to name its price?

**Already established:** Bowman’s Reborn wizard was a Crown battle mage and magistrate, obeyed atrocities under threats to his family, resisted, was killed, and returned with fragmented memories. His ring remains trapped on his finger. The DM now explicitly wants Halaster connected to his return.

**Interests:** corroborated memories, signet ring, surviving witnesses, atonement, Halaster and rebirth

**Guardrails:** Halaster’s role is a custom DM premise, not published lore or player knowledge. No mind control, automatic forgiveness, forced flashback behavior, reversible-life hostage switch, or surprise family resurrection. The Crown and family names remain open until established with Bowman.

### Redbrand hideout - A seal and a chance to act differently

**Trigger:** When an actual captive, surrender, or document gives a natural opportunity; do not force all three.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> A formal phrase catches at a memory: a desk, your hand, a seal pressed into wax. Nothing proves where it happened. What do you do with the feeling?

**Private preparation:** Offer the image privately and let Bowman accept, alter, or decline it. A generic official phrase is not proof the Redbrands served his Crown. Give him a present choice such as protecting a surrender or asking a frightened person what help they want.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Document the memory without treating it as evidence.
- Ask a neutral question about the phrase without naming his past.
- Set the memory aside and make the immediate situation safer.

**Evidence:** Label this a subjective memory. Later documents or independent witnesses must corroborate any factual detail before it enters confirmed history.

**Follow-through:** Record the present act separately from the memory. No clue requires him to repeat violence.

**If declined or missed:** If Bowman does not want a memory scene, offer the ethical choice without the image.

### Phandalin aftermath - The ring leaves an impression

**Trigger:** Only if Aldric chooses to examine or show the ring to someone he trusts.

**Suggested focus:** 6 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> The crest is damaged, but a careful drawing can preserve what remains. Who, if anyone, would you trust with a copy?

**Private preparation:** Create a rubbing or drawing without removing the ring or injuring the finger. Proposed craft clue: a small repaired notch inside the crest, distinct from a common Crown design. It suggests a commission that can later be checked; it grants no magic bonus.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Keep the copy private with a dated note.
- Send a limited image to an independent records specialist.
- Ask a companion to witness the examination without explaining everything.

**Evidence:** A crest match identifies a design or office, not its wearer or crimes. A maker’s commission and a separate appointment roll are needed to identify Aldric.

**Follow-through:** A reply can point to Waterdeep’s copy trade. Do not invent the Crown’s nation, his family’s names, or a full recovered biography.

**If declined or missed:** If he refuses an examination, a witness may later recognize his voice or an authenticated signature; the ring is not the only path.

### Wave Echo Cave - The echo in an old measurement

**Trigger:** Only after the party actually investigates the Forge; this is the first possible recognition signal.

**Suggested focus:** 8 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> For a moment, a remembered rhythm lines up with the pulse beneath the stone. Then it slips away. It is familiar, but familiarity is not an explanation.

**Private preparation:** DM premise: Halaster’s past return experiment used a resonance pattern from Undermountain. The Forge response can let Halaster recognize that his unidentified former subject is alive and traveling with this party. He was not following them from Session 1. This does not make Nezznar his agent.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Record the rhythm and ask another observer to describe it independently.
- Leave without investigating; a surviving record can provide the clue later.
- Tell one trusted companion the resemblance, without disclosing his whole history.

**Evidence:** The matching rhythm is an inference, not proof Halaster revived him. It needs an authenticated experiment record and a separate witness.

**Follow-through:** Offer a later message that admits uncertainty: “A result I believed lost may have endured.” It is a claim to investigate, not an order or proof.

**If declined or missed:** If no Forge interaction occurs, delay Halaster’s recognition until a credible Undermountain encounter or documented report reaches him.

### Waterdeep - Two records and a living witness

**Trigger:** After Aldric seeks records or consents to a contact; use a calm private meeting.

**Suggested focus:** 10 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> The archivist places a copy beside your sketch. “This tells us who held the office. It does not tell us what kind of person he was. For that, we need someone who was there.”

**Private preparation:** Proposed witness: Mera Vale, a former records clerk who survived the Crown’s service. She can attest to one dated order and one real intervention Aldric attempted, not his entire guilt or innocence. Let Bowman settle the Crown and family names before the documents name them.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Hear testimony without asking for absolution.
- Investigate a surviving family record separately from military records.
- Offer practical help chosen by someone harmed, with no demand for cooperation in return.

**Evidence:** Corroborate the appointment roll with a maker’s commission; corroborate Mera’s dated detail with an independent civilian record. A remembered face alone is insufficient.

**Follow-through:** Give one bounded recovered fact and one unresolved question. Atonement might fund aid, preserve testimony, escort a threatened witness, or return property after ownership is verified.

**If declined or missed:** If the witness declines contact, accept that boundary. Independent records and an intermediary still permit progress.

### Undermountain and the Nine - Halaster did bring you back

**Trigger:** After enough evidence to make the revelation earned, at an optional research site chosen alongside the published dungeon.

**Suggested focus:** 12 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> An experiment record carries three details a public rumor would not: the broken ring finger, the uneven scar, and the moment your breathing did not return. A later entry reads, “Continuity achieved. Subject departed. Memory incomplete.”

**Private preparation:** Custom DM truth: after the Crown disposed of Aldric, Halaster obtained his remains through an intermediary and used an experimental return lattice linked to Undermountain. The scorched signet helped identify the same individual during reconstruction; it is not a soul cage. The result was a Reborn with fragmented memory. Halaster studied survival of identity; he did not order the execution or the family’s murders. How the intermediary found the remains can be resolved later without rewriting the Crown.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Demand records and leave without accepting a debt.
- Negotiate a bounded exchange for further evidence with the party’s knowledge.
- Refuse contact and preserve independent proof of what happened.

**Evidence:** Use an authenticated dated lab record, corroboration from a separate surviving attendant or intermediary (proposed witness: a former mortal assistant, now independent), and a testable ring/record detail. Halaster’s testimony alone is not confirmation. Records grant no remote-control mechanism.

**Follow-through:** Halaster may call Aldric a success or claim ownership; that is his attitude, not a magical contract. He cannot switch Aldric off. Refusing him must leave a real path forward.

**If declined or missed:** If the party avoids Halaster, copies and an independent witness can establish his role. No forced audience is required.

### Late-campaign decisions - A life that is yours

**Trigger:** After the resurrection truth is supported and a concrete act of restitution is possible.

**Suggested focus:** 10 minutes; flexible, not a timer.

**Optional words to say:**

> The cause of your return is no longer the only question. There are people alive now who can say what they need. Which promise are you willing to make without asking them to forgive you?

**Private preparation:** Aldric can expose Crown records, protect witnesses, restore stolen property, help harmed communities on their terms, or refuse to recreate Halaster’s experiment. His restored life does not depend on continued service. Recovery may remain incomplete and meaningful.

**Choices and consequences:**

- Publish verified testimony with witness protection and acknowledge his own part.
- Commit to practical restitution chosen by affected people, without buying forgiveness.
- Secure or dismantle access to the experiment through an informed plan while preserving his independent existence.

**Evidence:** Use corroborated events, consent from living witnesses, and records of aid delivered. Do not promise that undoing a laboratory reverses death or restores his family.

**Follow-through:** Epilogue: one person is safer because of a promise he kept; a family name recovered is honored without becoming a demand for impossible restoration.

**If declined or missed:** If the group never reaches the experiment, let a smaller act of repair close the current chapter. His whole worth is not locked behind solving Halaster.

## Preparing the next Redbrand session

Before play, choose one or two hooks to stage. All five entrance-stage cards are available; they are alternatives, not obligations.

- Trista: a trust question without requiring a token reveal.
- Pebble: choose and mark a return route.
- Donavan: separate discovered claims from independently supported facts.
- Ringgo: look for a credible surrender, safe exit, or protective reconnaissance choice.
- Aldric: offer a private memory prompt only if Bowman wants it, then a present opportunity for restraint.

End by recording the actual decisions and who knows them. Leave every unused card prepared or deferred. New NPC working names can be changed in the card overrides before introduction.

## Using the tracker

1. Refresh the DM app and open Story > Character stories & spotlights, or use Character spotlights on a runbook card.
2. Set attendance for the selected session. Choose a campaign chapter and character. The five-person overview shows recorded played and offered moments, plus the last session with a played moment.
3. Expand Character direction and player preferences to record interests, themes to avoid, follow-up notes, or a pause. New preferences appear on that character's card.
4. Use the opening only when its trigger actually occurs. Adapt a card through the private overrides; the original remains recoverable. Suggested minutes are flexible preparation estimates.
5. Mark offered, deferred, or declined as appropriate. To mark played, write the actual response or outcome. Repeated clicks update the same session/card record rather than adding another spotlight count. Record an improvised moment separately when needed.
6. Review Recorded moments at the end of the session. A played entry can create a private, unapproved continuity draft. Review that draft's facts, evidence basis, sources, and who knows what before approval or sharing. A memory is not corroborated fact just because it was a successful scene.
7. Spotlight Undo/Redo affects tracker data; separately reviewed continuity records remain separate. Campaign backup and restore include tracker history, attendance, preferences, and card overrides.
