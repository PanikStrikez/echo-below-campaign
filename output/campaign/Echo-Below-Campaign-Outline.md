# The Echo Below — campaign outline

DM-only planning document • 28 September 2026

## Authority and use

This outline records the DM's latest approved story direction. It supersedes older proposals about Halaster's motivation and the selected character arcs. All future scenes below are planned possibilities, not completed campaign events. New names and specific staging details are proposed connective material. This document does not update the app or reveal anything to players.

Use alongside Continuity-Reference.md, Five-Character-Stories.md, Waterdeep-First-Expedition-Guide.md, and the supplied Dungeon of the Mad Mage. Published room keys, factions, geography, and encounter rules remain the dungeon's foundation; the scenes here are custom additions. Floor placements are opportunities, not mandatory destinations or session counts.

## The story in one paragraph

After resolving their unfinished business around Phandalin, five adventurers follow work and research to Waterdeep. Dracuseth introduces them to a shared courtyard, and contracts through the Yawning Portal fund their early expeditions. Beneath the city they encounter inhabited territories, rival explorers, and traces of abandoned experiments. Halaster proves a helpful, unsettling host who considers his guests available for research. The party gradually discovers that he intends to reconstruct his lost family’s entire environment, imposing it upon their present-day neighborhood. Knowledge from Undermountain and the satellite sites gives them several ways to respond, while the lives and relationships they build give that decision weight.

## Established foundation

- Ilyra, Halaster's spouse, was a bookbinder; Tavin, their adopted twelve-year-old child, made neighborhood maps and a crooked wooden model street with Halaster.
- Halaster was away when generations of incompatible civic wards activated together during an emergency. Ilyra helped maintain an evacuation passage; Tavin guided neighbors to it. They died when it collapsed.
- The collapse severed victims' magical identities from their names, remains, and possessions. Their souls cannot reliably be reached; their ultimate fate is unknown.
- Looking Glass amplifies resonance and makes temporary reconstructions. Contradictions with their surroundings destabilize them. Halaster responds by increasing the reconstructed area.
- A brief reconstruction of Ilyra recognized him, mentioned their private kettle joke, and asked after Tavin. This gives his hope evidence without proving who returned.
- His apparatus is connected to a present-day Waterdeep district. That physical connection makes it the site of his planned large experiment. The historical neighborhood need not have occupied the same location; its original location and date remain open.
- Aldric had identifiable remains and a recoverable soul. His independent survival encouraged Halaster but does not validate family restoration. His life is not dependent on the machine, ring, or continued cooperation.
- Halaster did not cause the Crown's crimes, direct Nezznar, or cause the original ward disaster. The knot's agency remains unconfirmed.
- There are nine sites total: Undermountain and eight satellites. They are not a transport network, and visiting all of them is unnecessary.

## Prologue: finish the business in Phandalin

The latest recorded stopping point is outside the Redbrand hideout. The party knows of the Black Spider and believes the Redbrands hold Gundren. DM truth: the Redbrands delivered him to Cragmaw Castle. The orc camp was killed; Hamun Kost disappeared. His whereabouts remain unknown.

Continue the hideout, castle investigation, and Wave Echo Cave according to player choices. Do not assume Gundren, Nundro, prisoners, Glasstaff, or Nezznar survive or die.

### Three opportunities to prepare the transition

1. **A lead, not a summons.** Nezznar's surviving notes, a copied mine record, or a living researcher can connect observations in the Forge to research in Waterdeep. Use two available sources; conversation with Nezznar is optional. The existing annotated map, THE ECHO BELOW, can carry one lead.
2. **An actual interaction.** If the party examines or manipulates a relevant Forge phenomenon, it can draw Halaster's attention remotely. Otherwise delay his recognition until a credible later report or encounter. No mandatory cinematic knowledge enters the party journal.
3. **A homecoming with an account to settle.** Run a Phandalin return reflecting actual rescues, losses, promises, and mine arrangements. Let the party choose who receives copies, who holds objects, and what help they owe. Travel to Waterdeep follows when they are ready.

Character openings: Trista chooses what to disclose about the BELOW token; Pebble preserves a sample or sketch with permission; Donavan writes an initial research question to Dianna; Ringgo can seek a legitimate introduction; Aldric can request a discreet records inquiry. These are invitations, not required actions.

Do not automatically award Echo's Whisper or merge it with Trista's BELOW token. Any transfer follows play. Begin the main Undermountain adventure when the actual party is appropriately prepared, around character level 5; no level is awarded by this outline.

## Act I: a place to return to

Suggested scope: arrival and the first three short expeditions, usually several sessions. Undermountain floors 1–3 become the early focus as readiness and routes permit.

### Arrival: Dracuseth opens a door

At the Yawning Portal, Dracuseth asks what happened in Phandalin before offering interpretations. He introduces nearby courtyard lodgings, helps arrange ordinary rental terms, and remains a Waterdeep contact rather than joining the adventuring party.

**Proposed courtyard name: Mending Court.** Place it within walking distance of the Portal, without requiring a street-map retcon. It shares a well, covered table, washing lines, and small workspaces. Dracuseth lives nearby and visits; he does not own the party's home or manage their choices.

Introduce only two neighbors at first. Add the others through ordinary encounters.

| Proposed resident | Independent life | First interaction | Later use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nessa Brindle, bookbinder | Wants steady orders and time to teach an apprentice | Needs help moving a heavy press | Can compare Ilyra's binding work with dated examples |
| Orenna Pike, retired porter | Organizes deliveries and argues about blocked access | Explains where equipment can safely be stored | Knows who needs assistance during an evacuation |
| Jori Fen and their adult brother Pellan, food sellers | Saving to lease a better stall | Offer an inexpensive shared supper | Their family preserves an account of an unnamed boy guiding an ancestor to safety |
| Aven Rusk, apprentice mage | Wants to pass an examination and stop working nights | Asks for quiet while practicing | Keeps useful observations of later disturbances, but makes interpretive mistakes |

The neighbors are custom proposals. Their occupations echo the themes without making them replacements for Halaster's family. Nobody is secretly an agent by default. Jori's family connection is one corroborated descendant account, not proof the whole courtyard shares that ancestry.

**First evening:** ask each character what they unpack, where they sit, and one small change they would like to make. Offer a shared meal without making attendance compulsory. Establish mundane geography before supernatural changes.

**Home develops in stages:** arrival gives names; first return gives recognition; a kept promise earns trust; a player-chosen improvement leaves a visible mark; later the residents ask for help. Do not burn the home down to manufacture attachment.

### Expedition 1: bring back a reliable route

**Offer:** through the Portal, Ilvara requests a verified survey of one bounded stretch of the first floor. Agree payment, turnaround conditions, and what counts as delivery. No requirement to clear the floor.

**Prepare:** the published entrywell and only the immediately reachable branches. The existing first-expedition guide supplies room details. Preserve lookouts, Undertakers, scouts, hazards, and inhabitants where the book places them; do not invent a safe path through keyed threats.

**Play:** descent, a navigation decision, one meaningful encounter, and a return. Negotiation or retreat can satisfy the contract if the survey honestly reports its limits.

**Character moments:** Pebble chooses route marks; Ringgo negotiates legitimate terms. Other players contribute normally.

**Clue:** a sensory comparison with Wave Echo Cave if someone notices or measures it. No required medallion and no instant explanation of the Nine.

**Homecoming:** a neighbor has kept a delivery for them. They can use part of their pay to improve their lodging. The map they actually made becomes an asset.

### Expedition 2: a recovery with competing claims

**Offer:** Orren at Ninepin Works asks for a missing survey instrument or an account of its fate. Place the custom object only after choosing a compatible room or current custodian on the party's known route.

**Complication:** another explorer or resident has a plausible salvage claim. Negotiation, buying it, exchanging information, or returning a documented failure can all advance the story.

**Rivals:** use the existing Gilded Compass—Kestrel, Pell, and Nym. They offer a map exchange, negotiate credit, and pursue their own contract. They neither automatically steal the prize nor rescue the party.

**Character moments:** a maker's mark links to Pebble's hammer-makers; Donavan obtains an ordinary sample useful for comparison. The mark suggests a lead, not immediate proof of a colleague's fate.

**Clue:** a research scrap with RESONANCE OBSERVED and an incomplete radial diagram. Preferred placement is the existing floor-2 overlay once accessible. If the expedition remains on floor 1, a legitimately recovered fragment can be discussed afterward; do not transplant a whole published room.

**Homecoming:** Dianna's first reply offers a testable question, not a solved mystery. Mara privately requests a conversation with Trista after acquiring a credible reason to know about her discovery.

### Expedition 3: make a route dependable

**Offer:** verify or negotiate a passage useful to more than one expedition. Choose its actual occupants and destination from the current floor map. It need not reach Skullport yet.

**Complication:** two groups need something incompatible from the passage—privacy, tolls, access, supplies, or protection. A sustainable agreement is an accomplishment. Violence can change control but does not automatically make the route safe.

**Character moments:** Ringgo's old contact sends a message requesting help leaving their former work. Aldric obtains the name of a records specialist able to examine the signet's provenance. Neither revelation requires a forced public confession.

**Clue:** the first clear sign of Halaster's personal attention: a courteous note with one accurate observation and one useful, bounded warning. He explains neither his project nor the party's importance.

**Homecoming:** hold a normal courtyard evening. Only afterward introduce the first small anomaly: for several breaths a patched wall shows a different doorway, then returns. Nobody is harmed. Two people observe it; a sketch establishes what was actually seen.

## Act II: useful guests, growing questions

Suggested scope: floors 3–8 and repeated city returns. Follow published access, factions, and hazards; no floor must be completed in a single visit.

### Three follow-up expedition shapes

- **Follow the maker:** Pebble investigates records of his hammer-makers' colleague. A workshop contact can be placed along an earned route without rewriting the Lost Level's Melairkyn history. Navigation challenges let his route notes help a real group return safely.
- **Follow the disagreement:** Donavan's rival proposes a shared investigation of an anomalous room. Dianna identifies a way to compare their predictions. Preserve separate measurements so disagreement can be resolved by evidence.
- **Follow the human cost:** a lead reaches an experiment survivor through Lantern House or independent expedition records. Arrange a meeting first; they are not a locked-room exposition reward.

At Skullport, Mara's buyer and Ringgo's contact can have business that intersects with published local power struggles. They need not work for the same employer. Establish what each knows and wants before involving the party.

### Halaster receives his guests

Build from a note to a short projection, then a substantive meeting around the existing floor-8 recognition opportunity, adjusted to actual contact. He offers seats, recalls a preference learned through observation, and gives genuinely useful help.

Original optional line: “You have been making yourselves quite at home. Good. People are much more interesting once they stop asking permission to exist.”

His first boundary violation is concrete and recoverable: he changes a passage during a survey to compare the party's responses. Show the change and allow rerouting; never secretly invalidate every map. If confronted, he treats the objection as a disagreement about hospitality. Record whether he honors a requested boundary later.

### The survivor

Proposed role: a former restoration technician who volunteered for a bounded test, was kept inside a reconstruction after asking to leave, and escaped with documented gaps in autobiographical memory. Give them a current occupation, relationships, and a practical request: obtain their records and locate another participant.

They know one experiment, not all of Halaster's history. Their account can be corroborated through a dated consent document and physical evidence. They favor dismantling the dangerous apparatus but can consider verified safe findings. Avoid equating damaged memory with unreliable moral judgment.

### Home begins to change

Escalate from the momentary doorway to a mismatched object, then a room briefly adopting an older arrangement. Keep an ordinary building survey and dated observations. Residents organize their own response and can reject experiments in their homes.

The cause remains an investigation. Anja can help people while arguing for isolation; Selise and Donavan's rival can favor cautious study. Reveal Mara's broker link to the Still Hand through existing invoices and testimony, not an automatic betrayal.

## Act III: the people inside the experiment

Suggested scope: floors 9–13, with a chosen satellite excursion when useful.

At Dweomercore, offer rival interpretations, records access, and an apprentice interested in restarting the work. Select that apprentice after checking the actual book cast and current outcomes; do not rewrite a published character's allegiance without preparation. Halaster's family's names and identities emerge separately from technical terminology.

The Silent Library, accessible through the Second Margin lead, supplies missing research and identifies nine total sites. Its vanished society remains part of the mystery. Modern custodians, neighboring users, or affected people provide present-day interests; do not fill the library with the supposedly vanished original members.

**Family evidence:** Ilyra's distinctive book repair, Tavin's annotated neighborhood map, and independent letters establish people with lives beyond Halaster. An archive or surviving collection can hold originals outside the dungeon; Undermountain supplies provenance and leads.

**Aldric:** pair an authenticated return record with a separate witness or intermediary and details matching his remains/signature. Confirm Halaster's role only when evidence supports it. Place the difficult Crown witness encounter during a city return so Bowman can choose timing and approach. Their evidence concerns a specific past act; their requested help concerns a present need.

**Donavan:** offer publication choices after there is something useful to publish. Warnings, methods, and personal names can be handled differently. Dianna and his rival may disagree in good faith.

**Trista:** make a token observation useful to a decision, with other routes to essential information. Let her decide whether to share privately, publicly, partially, or not at all.

**Ringgo:** his old contact's story contains an omission that matters, but assistance remains possible. Investigate who was harmed and what accountability could mean rather than mandating a fight.

Close this act when the party can identify Looking Glass as a restoration project and name Ilyra and Tavin. They may still disagree over whether a reconstruction is a returned person.

## Act IV: the cost of a larger world

Suggested scope: floors 14–20, keeping the published local adventures and factions central. Use unconnected dungeon problems and ordinary city life between major revelations.

- Recover evidence of the brief reunion. Preserve its exact limits: recognition, the private kettle memory, the question about Tavin, then collapse. Ilyra expressed no view about the current project.
- Compare a preserved piece or reliable drawing of Tavin's deliberately crooked model with Looking Glass's corrected version. The discrepancy demonstrates altered detail, not proof that every reconstruction lacks personhood.
- Civic records and separate survivor accounts reconstruct the ward disaster and evacuation. Jori's ancestor's account can identify Tavin only after another source supports the match.
- Find engineering evidence that the apparatus is coupled to their modern district. A survey plus an apparatus diagram establishes threatened boundaries; do not rely on Halaster telling the whole truth.
- At the Runestone-related research stage, distinguish amplification from the restoration application. Increased power explains expansion, not whether original souls return.
- Reveal a proposed test and observable preparation before imposing a deadline. Refusal does not make the experiment immediately activate; Halaster must secure another method or supplier, with traces the party can investigate.

All five characters can contribute: Trista examines access and custody; Pebble surveys material boundaries and routes; Donavan tests predictions; Ringgo follows suppliers and agreements; Aldric compares return evidence. None is a required key or sacrifice.

## The satellite menu: local lives and practical discoveries

These are proposed adventure functions, not finalized magical abilities. Each destination needs its own relevant inhabitants or affected community, independent dispute, return plan, and verified limits. Approximate level bands remain in Continuity-Reference.md.

| Site | Local concern | Possible contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Wave Echo Cave | Mine workers and custodians decide who may investigate | Amplification measurements and an independent baseline |
| Silent Library | Custodians and descendants dispute access to the vanished society's work | Detect gaps and contradictions in reconstructed records |
| Drowned Observatory | Sailors, salvagers, and coastal residents need safer waters | Recognize instability early; investigate Halaster's warning before adopting a method |
| Dead God's Scar | Residents and healers disagree about beneficial but unreliable effects | Document living consequences and propose safeguards for trial |
| Frozen Door | An earlier survivor and present guardians disagree over reopening | A tested isolation principle that can protect a limited boundary |
| Walking Tower | Stranded visitors or custodians need control over its unpredictable movements | Inspect a Looking Glass fragment and identify a reversible disconnection point |
| Broken Star | Explorers need a stable route out of shifting space | Test how a boundary fails, supporting evacuation and containment |
| Hollow Mountain | A community or relationship created through actual play | A late opportunity chosen around their unresolved problem; never the sole required solution |

Knowledge can also reach the party through consenting witnesses, recovered records, rival expeditions, or smaller local tests. A distant trip provides richer context and options; skipping it does not lock the finale.

## Act V: what they choose to preserve

Suggested scope: floors 21–23 and the city. Earlier choices affect allies, access, available safeguards, and how much people trust warnings. They do not force one ending.

Before the decision, provide three concrete briefs: the current activation plan, who and what it endangers, and each proposed intervention's tested limits. Unknowns should be named. Let residents decide how they participate; the party cannot promise a district's consent on its behalf.

| Available approach | What must be established | Consequence to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Stop or dismantle | Accessible interruption points and shutdown hazards | Protect residents, handle unstable remnants, decide research custody |
| Persuade Halaster | Credible evidence and an opportunity for actual dialogue | He must take observable action; sympathy does not cancel accountability |
| Limited rescue | A bounded method, independent safeguards, and willing participants | Contact or embodiment may fail; returned people have independent wishes |
| Respond during activation | Evacuation routes, warning arrangements, and reachable controls | Save lives and isolate damage even when complete prevention has failed |
| Preserve useful findings | A genuinely tested limited application and affected people's participation | Agree custody, access, monitoring, and a way to stop it |

Do not invent a guarantee that killing Halaster permanently ends the threat: his published rejuvenation matters. Separate dealing with him from securing the apparatus. Do not make destroying the knot a presumed available method.

Finish with a courtyard return shaped by actual outcomes, messages to Phandalin, and one player-chosen future for each character. Avoid prewriting forgiveness, family restoration, or retirement.

An accessible core clue is found through successful investigation; a roll can determine time, exposure, or extra detail instead of deleting the only lead. Missed evidence can exist in a plausible second source. Keep the source's knowledge limited and label claim, inference, and observation separately.

## Run the living campaign between expeditions

Use this short cycle:

1. **Choose:** offer two or three concrete leads and accept another destination.
2. **Prepare:** goal, known route, likely inhabitants, return conditions, two character opportunities.
3. **Explore:** run actual map choices and local conflicts; include at most one major mystery development unless the party actively pursues more.
4. **Return:** give space for recovery, relationships, and one ordinary home scene.
5. **Record:** who knows what, promises, discoveries, item custody, altered routes, surviving inhabitants, and unresolved requests.
6. **Advance:** select a few causally justified changes from faction plans, route conditions, Looking Glass preparations, reputation, and the Gilded Compass's expedition. Rumors announce important changes before they become unavoidable where possible.

Track spotlight with last substantial moment, the player's response, and an offered next opportunity. Rotate attention across several sessions; do not require a personal revelation every evening. Honor ignored hooks and revise from actual interests.
