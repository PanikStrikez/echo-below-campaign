# DM tools

## Campaign continuity at the table

Use Story > Campaign continuity & quests. Add an event with its session, source, status, and evidence basis. Write the separate player-safe wording, select who knows it, and link affected quests, map locations, promises, debts, rescues, escapes, or items. Review the shared preview before approving. Only facts and completed events change quest progress or tracked custody; rumors remain leads. Draft the next recap from approved events and review it before publishing.

For the current stopping point, see Session-02-03-Actual-Recap.md. Keep the Redbrand hideout prepared, not completed. Use continuity Undo/Redo to reverse record edits; this is separate from combat Undo.

## A simple session loop

1. Open with **one welcome**: food, a letter, a repaired item, a regular table, or a person remembering a promise.
2. State **one change**: an NPC's decision, new evidence, a cost, or an affected community.
3. Offer **up to two leads**. A lead should state destination, what is known, and what preparation would help.
4. End by recording a promise, a site condition, and what the party wants to do next.

## Fail forward

Use checks to change price, time, safety, precision, or attention - never whether the central clue exists. Show hazards before a roll. On a failure, give the lead and add one concrete consequence such as lost time, minor damage, a noisy approach, depleted supplies, or a rumor spreading. A sensible tool, spell, or physical plan should substitute for a roll.

## Evidence labels

Keep table talk clean by labeling discoveries:

- **Observed:** directly measured or witnessed.
- **Inferred:** a plausible conclusion from facts.
- **Claimed:** someone says it; their motive or evidence may be incomplete.
- **Open:** deliberately unanswered.

This makes the mystery fair. It also lets factions disagree without one of them being secretly foolish.

## Difficulty and attendance adjustments

The current party has five PCs: Trista, Pebble, Donavan Kemp, Ringgo Paul, and Aldric Vane. Treat satellite DCs and hazards as starting suggestions for the level and situation, not a numerical guarantee. Reassess after major level changes or when someone is absent. Do not solve low numbers with surprise extra combat. Instead, lower a DC by 2, add a safe method that takes time, give an allied NPC a logistical role, reduce damage, or turn a combat into a choice to escape, bargain, or secure a route. Use published creature statistics whenever a creature battle matters.

## NPC relationship clock

For each recurring NPC, mark **distant, known, trusted, strained, or opposed**. Change it only after an observable scene. Trust gives help that matches the NPC's role, not free solutions: Durnan gives a room and honesty; Selise gives research; Vess gives travel planning; Anja gives care and hard questions. Keep the city from becoming a quest-vending machine.

## The visual language

Warm human scenes use rain-dark slate, worn brass, cream paper, and amber windows. Echo scenes use restrained violet-gray light and doubled shadows. The selected campaign art direction is watercolor and gouache with ink edges and parchment texture. The images are campaign concept art rather than official portraits, maps, or binding canon.
